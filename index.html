01Autonomous vehicles2025 — 2026

Level-2 Autonomous BAJA Vehicle

Team Equinox · SAE BAJA India — Autonomous Head (2025) · Autonomous Head & Captain (2026)

Across two aBAJA SAEINDIA seasons I led the autonomy program for an all-terrain vehicle built from the ground up, competing in Lane Keep Assist, Autonomous Emergency Braking and Endurance events. In 2025 I was Autonomous Head; in 2026 I also captained the team, leading the mechanical, electrical, embedded and AI sub-teams to track readiness. The stack runs on ROS 2 Humble on an NVIDIA Jetson Orin Nano.

30 Hz
Unified LKA + AEB control loop
100 Hz
EKF vehicle-state estimation
~8×
Compute headroom for lane keeping on the Jetson*
  • Drive-by-wire in C++: a ROS 2 node that commands throttle, brake and steering over CAN and reads actuator and dashboard feedback every 20 ms.
  • Radar perception: decodes automotive-radar CAN frames with a DBC file and filters tracks with confirmation and miss thresholds, publishing them as custom RadarTrack messages.
  • AEB and ACC: a fuzzy-logic braking controller driven by time-to-collision, plus a safety-gated state machine that brakes when an obstacle enters an 8 m × 2 m danger zone. ACC uses a PID time-gap controller.
  • Lane keeping: a classical CV pipeline (bird's-eye warp, sliding-window search, polynomial fit) feeding Stanley / PID steering. I prototyped it in CARLA with Ultra-Fast Lane Detection, then validated it in IPG CarMaker 15.
  • Localization: EKF fusion of IMU, wheel encoders and steering encoder through robot_localization.

*Estimated in the team's hardware-compatibility report (≈4.2 ms per frame against a 33 ms budget).

02Aerial inspection · AI2024

AI Crack Detection with Autonomous Drones

Academic project — Project Lead & System Architect

An automated structural-inspection system. An autonomous PX4 drone flies inspection patterns around a structure while an edge-optimized deep-learning model finds cracks and measures them in real time. It feeds directly into AT Bots' AI-powered structural-inspection work.

40K
Concrete images in the training dataset
L · W · θ
Length, width and angle reported per crack
Edge
Model tuned for on-board inference
  • Pixel-level crack segmentation with a DeepCrack-style network in PyTorch (hierarchical features built on Holistically-Nested Edge Detection), trained on the METU concrete-crack dataset.
  • Crack quantification: contour analysis turns each mask into length, width, orientation and area, then renders a three-panel inspection report. A React web interface shows the results.
  • Optimized for the edge by trading inference latency against accuracy so it can run on resource-constrained on-board hardware.
  • Autonomous flight on PX4 + MAVROS + ROS 2, with inspection orbits prototyped in PyBullet and Gazebo. I led the AI, embedded and flight-control teams through integration.

03Aerial autonomy2024 — Present

ROS 2 Autonomous Drone Stack

AT Bots — Chief Technical Officer

The flight-software foundation for AT Bots' AI-powered inspection drones: a ROS 2 stack on PX4 and MAVROS for real-time control and navigation, hardened through simulation and failsafe testing.

3
Sensor streams fused: IMU, GNSS, LiDAR

  • PX4 offboard control from ROS 2 over uXRCE-DDS (px4_msgs) and MAVROS for autonomous take-off, trajectory tracking and landing.
  • Multi-sensor fusion of IMU, GNSS and LiDAR for accurate localization and obstacle avoidance.
  • GPS-failsafe research: a ROS 2 node that injects GPS failures into the PX4 sensor stream, detects the loss, and switches to GPS-denied navigation or return mode. I logged position, velocity and attitude to compare nominal and failure flights.
  • SITL validation in Gazebo and QGroundControl, including lost-offboard-link scenarios that fall back to position control.

04Legged robotics2024

12-DOF Quadruped Locomotion

Academic project — ROS 2 · Isaac Sim · Gazebo

A modular ROS 2 locomotion framework for a 12-DOF quadruped. It covers kinematics, gait generation and balance control, and was validated in high-fidelity physics before moving to hardware.

12
Actuated joints, 3 per leg
3
Controllers: stand, crawl, trot
2
Simulators: Isaac Sim and Gazebo
  • Per-leg inverse kinematics and body transformations for precise foot-trajectory planning and stable stance control.
  • Modular gait controllers (stand, crawl and trot) behind a state-command interface, with real-time joint synchronization through ros2_control and ramped joypad teleoperation.
  • IMU-based PID stabilization for posture correction on uneven terrain, tuned to cut oscillation and save energy.
  • Robot modelled in xacro / URDF and exported to USD for NVIDIA Isaac Sim, where I analysed joint torques and stability margins. A MoveIt configuration supports leg motion planning.
