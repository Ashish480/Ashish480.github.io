04Legged robotics · Flagship2024 — 2025

12-DOF Quadruped Robot: Locomotion from Simulation to Hardware

Main project — designed, built and programmed end to end

My main project: a four-legged walking robot built from scratch. I designed the locomotion stack in ROS 2, covering kinematics, gait generation and balance control, validated it in Isaac Sim and Gazebo, then brought it onto a real 3D-printed, servo-driven robot.

12
Actuated joints, 3 per leg
3
Controllers: stand, crawl, trot
Sim → Real
Isaac Sim / Gazebo to a physical robot
  • Physical build: 3D-printed body and legs with 12 servo-actuated joints (3 per leg), wiring harness and onboard control electronics.
  • Per-leg inverse kinematics and body transformations for precise foot-trajectory planning and stable stance control.
  • Modular gait controllers (stand, crawl and trot) behind a state-command interface, with real-time joint synchronization through ros2_control and ramped joypad teleoperation.
  • IMU-based PID stabilization for posture correction on uneven terrain, tuned to cut oscillation and save energy.
  • Robot modelled in xacro / URDF and exported to USD for NVIDIA Isaac Sim, where I analysed joint torques and stability margins. A MoveIt configuration supports leg motion planning.

01Autonomous vehicles2024 — 2026

Level-2 Autonomous BAJA Vehicle

Team Equinox · SAE BAJA India — Autonomous Systems Engineer → Autonomous Head → Captain & Finance Head

Over two aBAJA SAEINDIA seasons I grew from Autonomous Systems Engineer to Autonomous Head, and then to captain of Team Equinox. We built an all-terrain vehicle from the ground up to compete in Lane Keep Assist, Autonomous Emergency Braking and Endurance events. In 2026, as Captain and Finance Head, I led the mechanical, electrical, embedded and AI sub-teams to track readiness and managed the team's budget. The stack runs on ROS 2 Humble on an NVIDIA Jetson Orin Nano.

30 Hz
Unified LKA + AEB control loop
100 Hz
EKF vehicle-state estimation
~8×
Compute headroom for lane keeping on the Jetson*
  • Drive-by-wire in C++: a ROS 2 node that commands throttle, brake and steering over CAN and reads actuator and dashboard feedback every 20 ms.
  • Radar perception: decodes automotive-radar CAN frames with a DBC file and filters tracks with confirmation and miss thresholds, publishing them as custom RadarTrack messages.
  • AEB and ACC: a fuzzy-logic braking controller driven by time-to-collision, plus a safety-gated state machine that brakes when an obstacle enters an 8 m × 2 m danger zone. ACC uses a PID time-gap controller.
  • Lane keeping: a classical CV pipeline (bird's-eye warp, sliding-window search, polynomial fit) feeding Stanley / PID steering. I prototyped it in CARLA with Ultra-Fast Lane Detection, then validated it in IPG CarMaker 15.
  • Localization: EKF fusion of IMU, wheel encoders and steering encoder through robot_localization.

*Estimated in the team's hardware-compatibility report (≈4.2 ms per frame against a 33 ms budget).

02Edge AI · Aerial inspection2025 — Field-tested

Autonomous Structural Crack-Detection Drone

AT Bots — System integration & perception

A quadcopter flies an autonomous inspection mission, captures images of structural surfaces and detects cracks onboard with a deep-learning model I trained from scratch. The goal is to replace manual visual inspection with repeatable, documented surveys. Autonomous flights on real hardware and a field trial are complete.

Scratch
Custom model trained from scratch
Jetson

Field
Real-hardware flights and field trial done
  • End-to-end AI pipeline: collected and annotated images of structural surfaces, then handled preprocessing and augmentation, training, hyperparameter tuning and evaluation on a held-out test split.
  • Edge deployment: exported the model to an NVIDIA Jetson companion computer running live inference on camera frames. Postprocessing feeds a ROS 2 inspection logger, and contour analysis reports crack length, width and orientation.
  • Clean separation of roles: PX4 keeps flight stabilisation, position estimation and failsafes, and the Jetson runs perception, talking to PX4 over MAVLink (MAVROS / the PX4 ROS 2 bridge). The AI never drives the motors.
  • System integration of the quadcopter, PX4, Jetson, ROS 2 and QGroundControl, after simulation testing in PyBullet and Gazebo.

03Aerial autonomy2025

PX4 + ROS 2 Offboard Control & GPS-Failsafe

Robotics Research Centre, IIIT Hyderabad — Research internship project

A ROS 2 workspace that flies a PX4 quadrotor in offboard mode from Python and studies how the vehicle behaves when a GPS-loss or Return-to-Launch failsafe triggers.

FSM
Arm → take-off → offboard sequence
uXRCE
DDS bridge between PX4 and ROS 2
GPS-out
Failure injection and RTL / Hold tests
  • Offboard control from Python: a finite-state machine arms the vehicle, takes off and switches to offboard, with keyboard teleoperation of body-frame velocity and yaw rate plus a scripted elliptical-orbit mode.
  • GPS-failsafe experiments: two nodes inject GPS loss, push the vehicle into RTL or Hold, and log position, velocity and attitude to compare nominal and failure flights.
  • Tooling: connection diagnostics for the uXRCE-DDS bridge, RViz2 visualisation of pose, path and setpoints, and one-command bring-up of the agent, PX4 SITL, QGroundControl and all nodes.

07Machine learning · Intel UnnatiMay — Jul 2024

Heart-Attack Risk Prediction

Intel Unnati Industrial Training — selected for Intel's national program

An end-to-end machine-learning pipeline on structured clinical data that predicts heart-attack risk and explains which factors matter most. I built it during Intel's Unnati industrial training program.

  • Data pipeline: cleaning, z-score outlier detection and exploratory analysis of 303 patient records with 13 clinical features (age, chest-pain type, cholesterol, max heart rate and more).
  • Modelling: K-Means clustering to find patient groups, then decision-tree and logistic-regression classifiers, compared with proper validation.
  • Results: logistic regression reached 86.8% accuracy, F1 0.88 and ROC-AUC 0.87, with feature analysis that highlights the strongest risk indicators.
  • Presented findings and solution strategy to Intel mentors through structured reports and a demonstration.
